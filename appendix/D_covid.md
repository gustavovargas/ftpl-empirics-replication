## Appendix D. The COVID cross-section and the 2022 UK episode: data and variants

**Specification.** Cumulative inflation 2020–23 (per cent, Eurostat/OECD/World Bank) regressed on
(i) the raw COVID fiscal impulse (additional spending and forgone revenue, per cent of GDP, IMF
Fiscal Monitor, October 2021 vintage) and (ii) the Barro–Bianchi scaling: spending divided by the
product of the 2019 debt-to-GDP ratio and the 2019 average debt maturity (national debt
management offices and the ECB). Broad money growth is the cumulative growth of M3 (or the
closest national aggregate, FRED) over 2020–21, available only for countries with their own
currency.

**Variants, reported in full.** Full pool (n = 19): raw fiscal R² = 0.00; debt-scaled R² = 0.03;
Barro–Bianchi R² = 0.11. Euro-area members only (n = 7, one monetary policy): Barro–Bianchi
R² = 0.77 (r = +0.88). Non-euro countries with their own money (n = 7): broad money growth
R² = 0.43. The progression from 0.00 to 0.11 in the pool is not post-hoc fitting: the
Barro–Bianchi scaling is the theoretically motivated variable, published before this exercise.
Poland, an energy-price outlier, dominates the pool residuals. Maturity data are not perfectly
homogeneous across countries (bills included or excluded, swap adjustments); Switzerland's figure
could not be re-verified against the primary source.

**The 2022 UK episode.** Daily data: GBP/USD (FRED DEXUSUK, daily close), ten-year nominal and
real zero-coupon gilt yields (Bank of England IADB, IUDMNZC and IUDMRZC). Window 1 September to
14 October 2022; the mini-budget is 23 September, the Bank's intervention 28 September, the
U-turn 17 October. The intraday low of the pound (about 1.03 on 26 September, Asian session)
is below the daily close plotted in Figure 6. Real yields moved more than nominal yields in the
window — forced sales by leveraged pension funds were concentrated in index-linked gilts
(Pinter 2023) — so implied inflation compensation in this window mixes the mechanical and the
news components and is not used as a price-level measure here.
